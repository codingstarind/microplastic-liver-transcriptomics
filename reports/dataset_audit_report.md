## 3. Gene identifiers

- **55,359 identifiers are gene symbols** and **477 are unversioned Ensembl gene IDs** (`ENSMUSG…`). Every one of the 55,836 matches a `gene_name` in GENCODE M35; for the 477, GENCODE's name is the Ensembl ID itself.
- GENCODE M35 has 57,132 genes but only 56,897 distinct symbols. So the authors' transcript-to-gene table must have mapped to **symbols**, and:
  - **228 symbols in the file belong to more than one Ensembl gene** (e.g. *Ndor1*, *Pakap*, *Ptp4a1*). Their counts were presumably summed across genes. We cannot check this without the transcript-level files.
  - **1,061 GENCODE symbols are absent.** Most are small RNAs (miRNA, snoRNA, snRNA, rRNA) and pseudogenes, and **407 are on chrY**. These are probably genes absent from the transcript FASTA used by Salmon, or dropped while tximport was run. This is minor for mRNA analysis, but it is undocumented.
- **No duplicate identifiers.** An earlier version of our check reported 7 "duplicates after removing version suffixes". That was a bug in our code: it split symbols like *H2-M10.1* at the dot. It is fixed and covered by a test.

## 4. Values: what kind of numbers are these?

| Evidence | Observation | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Non-integer values | **0** (0%) | Not raw Salmon output, which is fractional. Not DESeq2-normalised counts, which are fractional. |
| Smallest non-zero value | 1 | Consistent with counts |
| Non-zero values below 1 | 0 | Rules out TPM and unrounded estimated counts |
| Column sums | 10.4 M – 15.5 M | Not TPM/CPM (would be 10⁶). Consistent with reads assigned to genes from ~15 M pairs/sample |
| Values as multiples of each column's minimum | 100% | Expected for integer counts |
| GEO label | "Raw counts" | — |
| GEO protocol | Salmon → tximport | — |

**Conclusion:** the values are **gene-level estimated counts from Salmon/tximport, rounded to integers**. This is most likely what `DESeqDataSetFromTximport()` or `round()` produces before DESeq2.

They are **not** raw read counts from an aligner plus featureCounts, and they are **not** normalised. Unresolved:

- Whether tximport used `countsFromAbundance = "no"`, in which case the matrix should be paired with an average-transcript-length offset, or a length-scaled option. Without the per-sample transcript-length matrix we cannot use the length offset that DESeq2 normally uses for tximport data.
- Whether rounding happened before or after summing transcripts to genes.

**Consequence:** the values are appropriate for count-based methods (DESeq2, edgeR, limma-voom) and for exploration after library-size normalisation. Gene-length bias across genes remains, so comparing *different genes* within a sample is only approximate. That does not affect comparing the *same* gene across samples, which is what we do.

## 6. Zeros and low expression

- **67.6%** of all matrix entries are zero.
- **29,651 genes (53%) are zero in all 19 samples**, and 41,986 are zero in at least one sample. This is expected for a whole-genome annotation that includes many non-liver genes, small RNAs (not captured by poly(A) selection) and pseudogenes.
- After the filter (≥ 10 counts in ≥ 4 samples), **13,777 genes remain**: 12,466 protein-coding and 839 lncRNA. They hold **99.86% of all counts**, and only 0.64% of their entries are zero.
- **Protein-coding genes** take 97.0% of counts. Mitochondrially encoded genes (chrM) take **7.4–11.9%** per sample.

## 8. Unexpected findings

1. **Library size is associated with PE treatment.** Every PE sample (10.4–12.8 M) has a smaller library than every vehicle sample (13.4–15.5 M). PE samples also have a slightly higher chrM fraction and fewer detected genes. PE libraries were indexed S48–S57, after all vehicle libraries (S39–S47).

   Possible explanations:
   - a technical batch (later extraction or library prep, or a different pooling ratio);
   - a biological effect on RNA content or composition (e.g. more mitochondrial transcripts);
   - chance.

   Size-factor normalisation corrects for depth, but if PE and vehicle libraries were processed differently, technical and treatment effects are **confounded and cannot be separated statistically**.

2. **Skeletal-muscle contamination.** Muscle genes are present in some samples (*Myh1*, *Acta1*, *Ckm*, *Mb*, *Ttn*, *Atp2a1*). Marker reads per million:

   | Sample | Muscle-marker CPM |
   |---|---|
   | MD_1 | 5,190 |
   | CD_MP_1 | 539 |
   | MD_2 | 316 |
   | MD_3 | 161 |
   | CD_2 | 58 |
   | All others | ≤ 20 |

   The most likely source is diaphragm tissue attached to the liver at dissection. MD_1 also carries traces of adipocyte genes (*Adipoq*, *Lep*, *Plin1*). This contamination drives PC1 of the primary PCA.

3. **Other composition differences.**
   - CD_MP_2 has high Kupffer/macrophage (573 CPM vs ~60–240) and erythrocyte markers.
   - MD_MP_2 and MD_MP_4 have higher cholangiocyte markers, and MD_MP_4 has higher stellate/fibrogenic markers.

   These could be real (inflammation, ductular reaction) or sampling differences, since the ~50 mg pieces may include different amounts of portal tract or blood.
4. ***Cfd* (adipsin) is ~100× higher in every MASH-diet sample** while true adipocyte markers stay at ~0. This looks like a liver response to the diet rather than fat contamination, but that needs checking (e.g. against the Xenium panel or the literature).
5. **Sex confirmed male:** *Xist* has 0–2 counts and *Ddx3y + Eif2s3y + Kdm5d + Uty* have 1,290–2,228 counts in every sample.

## 9. Unresolved dataset issues

| # | Issue | Why it matters | Possible resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GEO CD+Veh has n = 4; paper Fig. 1B says n = 5 per group | Unbalanced design; possible undisclosed exclusion | Ask the authors (contact: Tae Gyu Oh, OUHSC); check SRA run count |
| 2 | GEO extraction text says "four randomly selected" livers, which conflicts with n = 5 in three groups | Unclear how samples were selected for RNA-seq | Ask the authors |
| 3 | Library input: GEO says 500 ng, paper says "up to 1 µg" | Minor protocol inconsistency | Report as is |
| 4 | tximport settings and rounding not documented | Affects whether a length offset is needed in modelling | Reprocess FASTQs with Salmon ourselves (SRA SRX28316092–110) if needed |
| 5 | Symbol-level aggregation: 228 multi-gene symbols, 1,061 symbols missing (407 on chrY) | Small effects on specific genes | Reprocess from FASTQ if those genes matter |
| 6 | PE status confounded with library order (S48–S57) and smaller library size | Technical differences could look like PE effects | Cannot fully resolve. Check whether the PE signal persists after adjusting for library size; inspect SRA run dates / flowcells |
| 7 | Skeletal-muscle contamination in MD_1 (heavy), CD_MP_1, MD_2, MD_3 (moderate), CD_2 (light) | Inflates variance and dominates PCA; could create false DEGs for muscle-correlated genes | Sensitivity analyses: remove muscle-tracking genes, drop MD_1, add muscle score as covariate |

| 9 | Time of day and fasting state at sacrifice not reported | Lipogenic genes (*Fasn*, *Pnpla3*, *Me1*) are strongly circadian and feeding-dependent | State as a limitation; check circadian clock genes (*Arntl*, *Dbp*, *Per2*) across groups |
| 10 | GEO says "Chow Diet", but CD is a purified low-fat diet | Interpretation and comparability with other studies | Use "control diet" in our reports |
| 11 | Whether the 19 Xenium samples are the same mice as the 19 bulk samples | Linking bulk and spatial results | Check the Zenodo metadata |
| 12 | The phenotype data (ALT, TG, body weight, NAS) are not deposited per mouse | Cannot correlate expression with phenotype per animal | Check supplementary tables S1–S18; ask the authors |
