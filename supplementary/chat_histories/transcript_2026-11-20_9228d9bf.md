If those prefixes are the slides, slide is completely confounded with PE — the same problem you found in the bulk data. **Not confirmed**: the 419 MB `.h5` didn't finish downloading. **3. The PE evidence base is thin, and the one independent study partly disagrees.** Only Djouina 2023 (your PDF) is a substantive independent PE liver study. It **agrees** with Jung on *Ppara* up (~2-fold) but reports *Fasn*, *Acly*, *Acaca* **up** where Jung reports them down — in female mice, dietary route, no MASH arm. Its authors also conclude PE particles probably never reached the liver, so effects are gut-mediated. Three other papers support that indirect route.

**4. Reusable public data is scarcer than the papers imply.** Only three liver transcriptomes are reanalysable. One paper's sole accession (`PRJNA1305997`) is **not public**. ToxSci's Dryad deposit holds adipose gene lists only — no liver data. Eight papers generated omics and deposited nothing.

Two things I couldn't verify: the Xenium `.h5` contents, and three subscription papers — including the only one reporting that low-dose nanoplastics *reduced* liver damage, whose dose is never stated in the abstract.
