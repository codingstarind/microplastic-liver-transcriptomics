Task 1 — Literature Review and Research Gap Audit
I'm working on our BT3041 Analysis and Interpretation of Biological Data project at IIT Madras.
Our existing project investigates polyethylene microplastic exposure, liver gene expression, and metabolic dysfunction using mouse liver RNA-seq data (GSE294180).
Our original research questions are:
RQ1: Do polyethylene-associated gene-expression changes resemble those caused by a MASH-inducing diet?
RQ2: Does an already metabolically stressed liver respond differently to polyethylene exposure?
We can add new research questions, but they must remain connected to microplastics, liver biology, metabolism, gene expression or related molecular responses.
We've already performed QC, PCA, differential-expression analysis, pathway analysis and statistical sensitivity checks. However, several PE-associated results were sensitive to technical factors.
Before doing further analysis, I want a proper literature review. Do not write new analysis code or choose a new ML model yet.
Your assignment
1. Search for relevant primary research papers
Start with 10–15 substantial papers published up to November 2026, prioritizing:

* Microplastic exposure and liver transcriptomics.
* Microplastics combined with high-fat diets, MASLD or MASH.
* Studies identifying and experimentally validating molecular mechanisms.
* Studies reporting contrasting or negative effects.
* Computational models or omics analyses that we may be able to reproduce and evaluate.

Prioritize credible peer-reviewed original research over general reviews. Use reviews to identify additional primary papers.
Include our original Jung et al. 2026 Science Advances study and relevant independent PE, PS and PET studies.
2. Extract evidence from every paper
Prepare a comparison matrix containing:

* Title, authors, year, journal, DOI and publication link.
* Biological question and experimental design.
* Animal species, tissue and biological replicate counts.
* Polymer type, particle size, dose, exposure route and duration.
* Experimental groups and controls.
* Data types: RNA-seq, proteomics, metabolomics, histology, etc.
* Statistical and computational methods used.
* Main findings, including effect directions and statistical evidence.
* Whether findings were experimentally validated.
* Limitations explicitly acknowledged by the authors.
* Additional methodological concerns we should independently investigate.
* Public dataset accessions, downloadable files and code repositories.

Do not confuse computational predictions with experimentally validated findings.
3. Audit dataset availability
For every paper with public data, verify the actual GEO, SRA, Zenodo or other repository record.
Check whether the samples, biological groups, processed count matrices and metadata needed for reanalysis are available.
Distinguish verified availability from files that are merely mentioned in the manuscript.
4. Compare the papers
Look for findings that agree and disagree, particularly regarding lipid metabolism, PPAR signalling, inflammation, oxidative stress, mitochondrial function and interactions with metabolic dysfunction.
Determine which questions have already been answered convincingly, which have only preliminary evidence, and which might benefit from independent reproducibility testing.
Do not manufacture contradictions between studies with different experimental conditions.
5. Consider our BT3041 course
We have covered descriptive statistics, hypothesis testing, regression, model selection, cross-validation, classification, PCA and clustering.
Identify where these techniques could be scientifically useful in the literature, but don't force machine learning into the project.
I will also provide the ten course lecture PDFs for reference.
Deliverables
Create the following files in a `research/literature/` directory:

1. `literature_review.md` — a structured, properly cited review organized by scientific question, not just separate paper summaries.
2. `paper_matrix.csv` — the detailed comparison matrix.
3. `dataset_audit.md` — verified public data and what each dataset would permit us to analyze.
4. `research_gaps.md` — distinguish established gaps, possible limitations requiring verification, and unanswered questions. Do not finalize new hypotheses for us.

For every important factual claim, include a traceable source. Provide DOI links and, where possible, the relevant paper section, table or figure. Clearly mark anything you could not verify.
Important instructions
Do not fabricate references, sample counts, statistical results, dataset availability or validation claims.
Do not conclude that a paper is flawed simply because it uses a method different from ours.
Do not recommend a final dataset, start implementing models, or establish our new hypotheses independently.
I want a rigorous evidence base that our team can read, verify and use to make our own research decisions.
Start with the literature search and evidence extraction. Report your findings clearly so we can discuss them before proceeding to analysis.
