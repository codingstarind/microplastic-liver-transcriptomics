## Biological question
Whether chronic oral exposure to 5-um polystyrene microspheres (PS-MS) worsens HFD-induced obesity, and whether the gut microbiota mediates it. Objectives: "We aimed to investigate the effect of PS-MS exposure on high-fat diet (HFD)-induced obesity and underlying mechanisms." The mediation question is addressed by antibiotic depletion (ABX) and fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT).

## Experimental design (groups, controls, randomisation/blinding if stated)
Three separate 24-mouse experiments ("Seventy-two male C57BL/6J mice (24 mice per experiment)"):
1. Main 2x2: ND, ND+PS-MS, HFD, HFD+PS-MS; n=6 per group, 8 wk.
2. ABX experiment: the same 2x2 but every mouse also received a daily broad-spectrum antibiotic cocktail by gavage for the whole 8 wk (ampicillin 100, neomycin 100, metronidazole 100, vancomycin 50 mg/kg BW; n=6 per group). This is a microbiota-depleted replicate of the 2x2, used as a mediation test.
3. FMT experiment: donors fed HFD or HFD+PS-MS (n=6/group); ABX-pretreated (1 wk) HFD-fed recipients gavaged 3x/week for 8 wk with donor slurry; groups R-HFD and R-HFD+PS-MS (n=6/group). Donor feces "pooled over the course of the 8-wk experiment".
- Housing: "Groups of mice were housed together (n=6 per group, 3/cage)" - so CAGE IS NOT THE UNIT OF ANALYSIS; 2 cages per group, 3 mice per cage, and mice were analysed as independent units.
- Baseline standardisation: "all groups of mice were co-housed for a period of 1 wk prior to the commencement of the experiment" (good for microbiome baseline).
- Controls: ND and HFD groups received "filtered and autoclaved water"; PS-MS groups received water with 10 mg/L PS-MS, replaced every 2 d. No particle-free vehicle/surfactant control beyond plain water.
- Randomisation: stated ONLY for the FMT experiment - "The mice were randomly divided into four groups (n=6)" (Fig. 6 legend). Not stated for the main or ABX experiments.
- Blinding: not reported anywhere (searched "blind"); MASLD scoring, goblet-cell counts and IF intensity are all operator-scored.

## Species/strain/sex/age; tissue(s)
- Mouse, C57BL/6J, male only, 6-8 wk old at purchase (Vital River): "Seventy-two male C57BL/6J mice (24 mice per experiment), aged 6-8 wk". Fig. 1A says exposure "started at 8 wk of age". Housing 24+/-1 C, 55+/-5% RH, 12 h light:dark.
- Tissues collected and weighed: liver, distal colon, inguinal (iWAT), epididymal (eWAT) and perirenal (pWAT) white adipose tissue; plasma/serum; feces.
- Assayed tissues: liver (H&E, Oil Red O, MASLD score, liver TC/TG), distal colon (AB/PAS goblet cells, Muc2/Tjp1 qPCR, MUC2 ELISA, TJP1 and occludin immunofluorescence), iWAT (H&E adipocyte area), serum (lipids, LPS, cytokines), feces (16S).
- IMPORTANT FOR THE TEAM: there is NO liver molecular work at all - no liver RNA-seq, no liver qPCR, no liver protein. All gene expression in this paper is COLON only (Muc2, Tjp1).

## Biological replicates per group
- Main 2x2 phenotype (Fig. 1): n=6 per group, "Each dot represents one mouse".
- MASLD activity score: n=5 (Fig. 1G legend); Oil Red O staining "livers from 4-5 mice per group were used for staining" (Methods, Histology).
- 16S rRNA (Fig. 2): n=5 per group ("Data are represented as mean+/-SD with scatter plots (n=5)").
- Colon barrier/inflammation (Fig. 3): n=6 unless indicated; TJP1/occludin immunofluorescence quantification n=3 (Fig. 3F,G).
- ABX experiment (Figs. 4-5): n=6 per group; MASLD score n=4 (Fig. 4G); IF n=3 (Fig. 5F,G); AB/PAS goblet cells reported as "Each data represents 1 crypt, 3 slices/group, 3 crypts/slice (n=9)" - i.e. n=9 CRYPTS, not 9 mice (pseudoreplication to flag).
- FMT experiment (Figs. 6-7): n=6 per group; MASLD score n=4 (Fig. 6G); goblet cells again n=9 crypts (Fig. 7A).
- Note the total-animal arithmetic: 24 mice per experiment = 4 groups x 6. For the FMT experiment, 24 would have to cover both donors (2x6) and recipients (2x6), which exactly fits but leaves no spare animals.

## Polymer; particle size; supplier; label; characterisation; endotoxin
- "An aqueous suspension containing PS-MS at a concentration of 25 mg/mL was procured from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. The suspension consisted of beads with a particle size of 5 um (ranging from 4.0 to 4.9 um, identified as L815942) and were dispersed in deionized water at a concentration of 5.0% wt/vol." (Methods, Characterization of PS-MS). Note the internal mismatch: nominal "5 um" but the stated range is 4.0-4.9 um.
- Surface chemistry: not reported (no COOH/NH2/plain statement). No fluorescent label (so no tissue-deposition imaging in this study, unlike ehp2023).
- Characterisation performed: transmission electron microscopy morphology only (Hitachi HT7800, Figure S1 - not read). No measured size distribution/DLS, no zeta potential, no FTIR/Raman confirmation of polymer identity, no additive/leachate analysis.
- Dispersion: "all suspensions underwent thorough sonication, followed by dilution with water prior to their use"; working suspensions sonicated 10 min; "Bottles were shaken to ensure an even distribution of the microspheres in the drinking water to prevent sedimentation" - but no verification of the actual delivered concentration over the 2-d interval (contrast with ehp2023, which did check).
- Endotoxin: NOT reported (searched "endotoxin" - only hits are about Desulfovibrio-derived LPS and metabolic endotoxemia in the Discussion). This matters here because the paper's central readout is serum LPS.

## Dose; route; vehicle; frequency; duration
- "Suspensions containing 10-mg/L PS-MS were prepared using ultrapure sterile water, followed by sonication for 10 min"; "the ND + PS-MS and HFD + PS-MS groups received water supplemented with PS-MS at a dose of 10 mg/L per day. PS-MS water was replaced every 2 d. This treatment regimen was followed for a duration of 8 wk." Route: oral, drinking water, ad libitum.
- Per-animal dose, derived by the authors in the Discussion: "It was estimated that each control mouse consumed 3.6 mL of water/d in this study, resulting in a PS-MS intake of 36 ug/d per mouse", which they equate to "the minimum potential exposure dose for humans (0.1 g of MPs/wk)". For a ~20 g mouse this is ~1.8 mg/kg BW per day.
- Water intake was measured every 2 d and "There was no significant difference in daily water consumption in mice exposed to ND or HFD with and without PS" (Results, Fig. S2) - so dose was comparable across PS groups.
- Single dose level; no dose-response. Duration 8 wk (vs 4 wk in ehp2023).

## Diet / metabolic-stress model
- ND: "a normal diet (ND; D12450B, 3.85 kcal/g; Research Diet, Inc.) containing 10% kcal from fat".
- HFD: "a HFD (D12492, 5.24 kcal/g; Research Diet, Inc.) consisting of 60% kcal from fat". Compositions in Table S2 (not read).
- NOTE this is a properly MATCHED purified control (D12450B is the standard low-fat match for D12492) - a methodological strength relative to the 2023 chow-controlled study.
- Model: 8-wk diet-induced obesity with MASLD-range liver histology (MASLD activity score by Kleiner criteria: steatosis 0-3, ballooning 0-2, lobular inflammation 0-3 as defined in Methods). No MASH-inducing additions (no fructose, no cholesterol, no CDAHFD), so steatosis-dominant rather than full steatohepatitis/fibrosis.
- Food intake was ad libitum and NOT pair-fed; notably HFD+PS-MS mice gained more weight "despite consuming less high-fat feed and calories" (Results, Fig. 1D).

## Data types
- No transcriptomics of any kind (no RNA-seq, no microarray). Gene expression = SYBR-based RT-qPCR of colon Muc2 and Tjp1 only, 2^-ddCt, normalised to Gapdh, primers "designed and saved by our laboratory" (Table S1).
- 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing: V3-V4, primers 338F/806R, barcoded, Illumina MiSeq PE300 (Allwegene, Beijing); feces collected from each individual mouse every 4 wk.
- Protein: MUC2 ELISA (colon homogenate); serum LPS, TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-1beta, IL-17A ELISAs (all Jiangsu Meimian kits); immunofluorescence for TJP1/ZO-1 and occludin in distal colon (confocal, ImageJ intensity).
- Histology: liver H&E + Oil Red O + MASLD activity score; iWAT H&E adipocyte area; distal colon AB/PAS goblet cells.
- Biochemistry: liver TC and TG; serum TC, TG, LDL-C, HDL-C (Nanjing Jiancheng kits); oral glucose tolerance test (2 g/kg, 6 h fast, 0-120 min, AUC).
- Interventions used as evidence: ABX depletion (">97% of the intestinal bacteria were eradicated", Figure S4) and FMT with 16S confirmation of engraftment (Figure S5, PCA).

## Statistics & computational methods
Verbatim (Methods, Statistical Analysis): "Analysis of the data was performed using GraphPad software (version 9.0). Unless otherwise indicated, the parametrically distributed data underwent Student's t-test for comparisons between two groups, whereas nonparametrically distributed data were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney test, and differences in mean values among more than two groups were determined using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with a Tukey multiple comparison test. The Brown-Forsythe and Welch ANOVA test was performed when the variances were not equal. Multiple unpaired t-tests and two-way repeated measures ANOVA with a Tukey multiple comparison test were performed as indicated in figure legends. Correlation statistical analyses are performed by Pearson's correlation by using R (version 3.5.3 ...). Statistical significance was considered when p<0.05."

CRITICAL FOR THE TEAM - was MP x diet tested as a formal interaction? NO.
- The four groups of the 2x2 were compared by ONE-WAY ANOVA + Tukey across groups (Figs. 1, 2, 3 legends: "Data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA with a Tukey multiple comparison test"). There is no diet x PS-MS factorial model and no interaction p-value reported anywhere.
- The only "two-way" analysis in the paper is "two-way repeated measures ANOVA" on the body-weight-over-time curves (Fig. 1B, Fig. 4B) - the two factors there are GROUP and TIME, not diet and PS-MS; the legend reports per-time-point Tukey contrasts (e.g. "###p<0.001 ... HFD + PS-MS vs. HFD").
- The central claim ("PS-MS exacerbated HFD-induced obesity ... more pronounced in HFD-fed mice but not in ND-fed mice") is therefore again based on significance in one diet and non-significance in the other, not on a tested interaction.
- Mediation claims (ABX, FMT) are made by comparing a SEPARATE ABX cohort in which the PS effects were absent, rather than by a formal diet x PS x ABX three-way model or a mediation analysis.
- Correlations: Pearson in Methods but the Discussion says "using Spearman's correlation" (Discussion, para on correlation analysis) - INCONSISTENT. Figure 3M legend says "Pearson correlation values were used for the matrix". No multiple-testing correction is reported for the 15 families x 17 phenotypes correlation heatmap (~255 tests).
- 16S pipeline: Pear 0.9.6 read merging, Vsearch 2.7.1 chimera removal, Uparse/Vsearch 97% OTUs, BLAST taxonomy, QIIME 1.8.0 alpha diversity (Shannon, Simpson, Chao1), PCA in R 3.6.0, Metastats and LEfSe for differential abundance. LEfSe LDA threshold and p-value criteria are not stated (contrast with ehp2023 which stated LDA>2, p<0.05). No FDR statement for Metastats/LEfSe.
- No power calculation, no pre-registration, no exclusion criteria, no cage effect modelling despite 3 mice/cage.

## Main findings with effect directions and statistical evidence
Experiment 1 - main 2x2 (all pairwise one-way ANOVA + Tukey, n=6):
- Body weight: NO PS effect under ND ("mice treated with PS-MS gained a similar amount of weight under ND"); under HFD, "the HFD + PS-MS group gained >17% more BW (BW relative to week 0: 158.17%+/-8.25 vs. 175.21%+/-5.35; Figure 1B,C)". VALIDATED (direct measurement). This is the one quantitative effect size given in the body text.
- Food/energy intake: HFD+PS-MS gained more weight "despite consuming less high-fat feed and calories" (Fig. 1D) - i.e. the weight gain is NOT explained by hyperphagia. Water intake not different (Fig. S2).
- Liver weight: no difference ND vs ND+PS-MS; HIGHER in HFD+PS-MS vs HFD (Fig. 1E). VALIDATED.
- Liver histology: HIGHER MASLD activity score and larger Oil Red O lipid area in HFD+PS-MS vs HFD (Fig. 1F-I; n=5 and 4-5). VALIDATED (histology).
- Liver lipids: liver TC and TG HIGHER in HFD+PS-MS vs HFD (Fig. 1P,Q). VALIDATED (biochemical).
- Adipose: iWAT +76%, pWAT +64%, eWAT +39% vs HFD; larger iWAT adipocytes; no differences under ND (Fig. 1J-N). VALIDATED.
- Glucose: fasting glucose and OGTT AUC HIGHER in HFD+PS-MS vs HFD; "no significant difference in fasting blood glucose levels and blood glucose AUC between the ND group and the ND + PS-MS group" (Fig. 1O). VALIDATED.
- Serum lipids: TC, LDL-C and LDL-C/HDL-C HIGHER in HFD+PS-MS vs HFD; NULL for serum TG and HDL-C ("There was no difference observed in TG and HDL-C with PS-MS supplementation when compared with the HFD group", Fig. 1R,U).
- Microbiota (Fig. 2, n=5): Chao1 and observed species HIGHER with PS-MS in BOTH ND and HFD (so this PS effect is NOT diet-restricted); Shannon showed "no significant difference among the ND, ND + PS-MS, and HFD groups" (Fig. S3B) - a null the authors highlight. Firmicutes:Bacteroidetes ratio LOWER in HFD+PS-MS (<half of HFD) - note this is OPPOSITE to the classic obesity-associated high F:B, and the authors acknowledge the obesity was "independent of the F:B ratio". Family level: Lachnospiraceae, Oscillospiraceae, Marinifilaceae, Akkermansiaceae, Deferribacterales UP; Bifidobacteriaceae and Atopobiaceae DOWN. Genus: Desulfovibrio UP, Bifidobacterium and Parabacteroides DOWN. Desulfovibrio rose "in the HFD, but not the ND groups". OMICS-ONLY (16S), though downstream causality is addressed by FMT.
- Colon barrier (Fig. 3): goblet cells, Muc2 mRNA, MUC2 protein, Tjp1 mRNA, and TJP1/occludin protein ALL LOWER with PS-MS "regardless of whether they were fed a ND or a HFD" / "in both the ND and HFD groups". IMPORTANT: this core barrier effect is NOT diet-dependent - it occurs under normal diet too. VALIDATED (histology + qPCR + ELISA + IF, three independent modalities).
- Serum LPS and cytokines (Fig. 3H-L): LPS, IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, IL-17A "significantly higher in the HFD + PS-MS group than in the ND group". NOTE the stated contrast is HFD+PS-MS vs ND, NOT vs HFD - so the text does not establish that PS-MS added to HFD alone for these cytokines. This is a weaker claim than the abstract implies. VALIDATED (ELISA) but contrast choice should be checked against Table S3.
- Correlations (Fig. 3M): Lachnospiraceae, Oscillospiraceae, Desulfovibrionaceae, Marinifilaceae, Streptococcaceae positively correlated with BW gain, fat mass, serum/liver lipids, glucose, cytokines and negatively with MUC2; Bifidobacteriaceae, Atopobiaceae, Akkermansiaceae, Prevotellaceae, Muribaculaceae opposite. COMPUTATIONAL (correlation only, uncorrected).
  Internal inconsistency to flag: Akkermansiaceae was reported as INCREASED by PS-MS under HFD (Fig. 2J) yet "f__Akkermansiaceae showed significant negative associations with BW gain, liver weight, epididymal adipose, fasting glucose, and serum TC" - the taxon moves in the direction opposite to the phenotype it predicts.

Experiment 2 - ABX (microbiota-depleted) replicate of the 2x2: a set of deliberate NULLS used as the mediation evidence.
- ">97% of the intestinal bacteria were eradicated (Figure S4)". "the PS-induced obesity outcomes were not detected in mice fed with either the ND or the HFD after receiving ABX treatment" - no PS effect on weight gain, liver weight, liver fat, iWAT/eWAT/pWAT, adipocyte size or serum lipids (Fig. 4C-R). VALIDATED-BY-INTERVENTION (antibiotic depletion), but note this is an absence of effect in a separate cohort, not a tested three-way interaction.
- Barrier readouts also became null under ABX: goblet cells, Muc2 mRNA/protein, Tjp1 mRNA, TJP1 and occludin protein all "not significantly different between mice exposed to PS-MS in both diet groups" (Fig. 5A-G).
- Serum cytokine differences also disappeared under ABX (Fig. 5H-K).
- CONTRARY / awkward results inside the ABX arm, reported by the authors: food and energy intake were significantly HIGHER in ABX HFD+PS-MS than ABX HFD (Fig. 4D) yet body weight did not differ; and "the blood glucose AUCs in the ND + PS-MS and HFD + PS-MS groups were significantly lower than in the ND and HFD groups, respectively" (Fig. 4M) - i.e. under ABX, PS-MS was associated with BETTER glucose AUC, a direction reversal that the Discussion does not address.
- ABX did not abolish the HFD effect itself: "ABX treatment was not completely resistant to HFD-induced weight gain compared with the ND group" (Results, Fig. 4B,C).

Experiment 3 - FMT (HFD recipients, n=6/group, Student's t-test):
- Engraftment confirmed by 16S PCA (Fig. S5).
- R-HFD+PS-MS vs R-HFD: HIGHER body weight/gain, liver weight, MASLD score, iWAT/eWAT/pWAT mass, adipocyte area, serum TC, LDL-C, LDL-C/HDL-C (Fig. 6B-O). Food and energy intake NOT different (Fig. 6D) - so the transferred phenotype is not intake-driven. VALIDATED-BY-INTERVENTION (microbiota transfer).
- NULL inside the FMT arm: fasting glucose higher but "there was no significant difference in blood glucose AUC between the R-HFD group and the R-HFD + PS-MS group" (Fig. 6M); the authors flag this.
- Colon: fewer goblet cells, lower MUC2 protein, lower Muc2 and Tjp1 mRNA in R-HFD+PS-MS (Fig. 7A-D). Serum TNF-alpha and IL-17A higher, "However, no difference was found for IL-6 (p=0.086)" (Fig. 7E-G) - explicit non-significant result with its p-value.
- The FMT arm uses only HFD recipients; there is no ND-recipient arm, so the microbiota-mediated effect was never tested on a normal-diet background.

## Pathway-theme directions
- Lipid metabolism / lipogenesis: phenotypic only - liver TC and TG UP, hepatic Oil Red O area UP, WAT mass UP, serum TC/LDL-C UP in HFD+PS-MS vs HFD. NO lipogenic gene or protein measured (no Fasn, Scd1, Srebf1, Acly, Pparg, Cd36). Direction of the pathway is inferred from lipid content, not from expression.
- PPARalpha / fatty-acid oxidation: NOT MEASURED.
- Cholesterol synthesis: not measured mechanistically; liver and serum total cholesterol UP.
- Inflammation: serum TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-1beta, IL-17A and LPS UP (ELISA) in HFD+PS-MS; abolished by ABX; partly transferred by FMT (TNF-alpha, IL-17A up; IL-6 ns). No hepatic inflammation markers, no NF-kB, no NLRP3, no macrophage markers; liver inflammation only as the lobular-inflammation component of the MASLD score.
- Oxidative stress: NOT MEASURED (no ROS/MDA/SOD/GSH/Nrf2/Hmox1).
- Mitochondrial function: NOT MEASURED.
- Fibrosis: NOT MEASURED - the Kleiner-based MASLD score used here is defined in Methods as steatosis + inflammation + ballooning only, with no fibrosis stage.
- Circadian: NOT MEASURED; sacrifice time of day not stated (only "overnight fasting period before cervical dislocation").
- Barrier/mucus: Muc2/MUC2, Tjp1/ZO-1, occludin all DOWN with PS-MS in BOTH diets.
- MP x diet interaction: asserted ("exacerbated", "superimposed effect between PS-MS and HFD") but NEVER formally tested; one-way ANOVA + Tukey only. And the picture is mixed: obesity/liver/glucose effects were HFD-restricted, whereas colon barrier damage and increased Chao1 richness occurred under ND as well.

## Author-acknowledged limitations
There is no dedicated "Limitations" section; the following are stated inline in the Discussion:
- Incomplete microbiota depletion: "However, this investigation had certain limitations, most notably our inability to fully eradicated the intestinal microbial population." (Discussion, ABX paragraph).
- Germ-free would be stronger than FMT into ABX mice: "Transplanting the gut microbiota to germ-free mice would provide more compelling evidence to support these findings." (Discussion, FMT paragraph).
- Unexplained FMT null: "significant differences were observed only in fasting blood glucose levels, not in blood glucose AUC, potentially due to disruptions in the initial microbiome conditions, such as routine and sterile conditions." (same paragraph).
- Housekeeping-gene caveat: "A study has shown that a HFD affects the expression of the housekeeping gene Gapdh in liver and adipose tissues. However, it remains unclear whether a HFD affects the expression of Gapdh in the intestine. Although this experiment did not employ different housekeeping genes for verification, the results have been validated at both the gene and protein levels" (Discussion, barrier paragraph).
- Generalisability across particle types/sizes: "Previous studies have reported that oral intake of 0.45-0.53 um PS-COOH ... beads or a combination of PS-MS with varying particle sizes ... led to a reduction weight in HFD-fed mice. This implies that PS-MS with distinct particle sizes and properties may have dissimilar effects on BW." and "In the following phase, we will investigate the impact of PS-MS and other microplastic particles on obesity in mice under varying dietary conditions and dosage levels."
  (NOTE: the first of those cited contrasting studies is the 2023 EHP paper, which used exactly 0.45-0.53 um PS-COOH - the two papers in this batch disagree on the direction of the body-weight/liver-weight effect. ehp2023 reported no body-weight change and LOWER absolute liver weight in HFD+MP; ehp2024 reports HIGHER body weight and HIGHER liver weight.)
- Dose-dependence is non-monotonic per cited work: "relatively low doses of PS-MS were found to trigger obesity, whereas higher doses did not" - the authors accept their single dose cannot resolve this.
- No MP effect on obesity under ND is explicitly acknowledged: "we report that administering 10-mg/L PS-MS in drinking water did not induce obesity in mice on a ND."

## Methodological points to verify independently
1. Interaction: the design is 2x2 (and effectively 2x2x2 with ABX) but every group comparison is one-way ANOVA + Tukey. Does Table S3 give enough per-animal data to fit a diet x PS-MS model and recover an interaction p-value? Would the obesity outcomes still show a significant interaction?
2. Cage: 3 mice/cage, 2 cages/group, analysed as n=6 independent mice. For a microbiota-driven phenotype, cage is a strong confounder (coprophagy homogenises cagemates). Was cage recorded, and is group confounded with cage? No cage-level random effect is used.
3. n inconsistencies: phenotype n=6, 16S n=5, MASLD score n=5 (main) or n=4 (ABX, FMT), Oil Red O n=4-5, IF n=3. Which mice were dropped and why? No exclusion criteria are given.
4. Pseudoreplication: goblet-cell counts are reported as "n=9" crypts (3 slices, 3 crypts/slice) in Figs. 5A and 7A. Are these 9 crypts from how many mice? If from 3 mice, the n is 3, not 9.
5. Serum cytokine contrast: the text states the cytokine/LPS elevations as "HFD + PS-MS group than in the ND group". Is HFD+PS-MS significantly above HFD itself? Check Table S3 - this determines whether inflammation is a PS effect or mainly a HFD effect.
6. ABX arm direction reversal: PS-MS-exposed ABX mice ate MORE but had a LOWER glucose AUC than their diet controls. Is this a real protective effect, an artefact of the antibiotic model, or a labelling issue? Unaddressed in the Discussion.
7. The ABX cohort is a separate experiment, not a randomised ABX x PS factorial within one cohort; batch/time effects between the three 24-mouse experiments cannot be excluded. Were the three experiments run concurrently?
8. Pearson (Methods and Fig. 3M) vs Spearman (Discussion) - which was used, and is there any multiplicity correction over ~255 correlations?
9. Particle characterisation: no size distribution, no zeta potential, no polymer confirmation, no surface chemistry, no leachate/additive or surfactant analysis, and no endotoxin testing - the last is important because serum LPS is a primary outcome and commercial bead suspensions can carry endotoxin.
10. Delivered dose: was the PS-MS concentration in the bottles verified over the 2-d replacement interval (sedimentation/wall adhesion of 5-um beads is likely)? The 36 ug/d figure is derived from an assumed 3.6 mL/d intake.
11. No particle-free vehicle control matching any suspension aids; control water was "filtered and autoclaved", the PS water was "ultrapure sterile" - slightly different water handling between arms.
12. Blinding for MASLD scoring, adipocyte area, goblet counts and IF intensity is not stated; all are operator-dependent.
13. FMT donor feces were "pooled over the course of the 8-wk experiment" and pooled across donors - so donor-level variation is lost and recipients within a group are not independent of the shared inoculum.
14. Sex: males only, as in ehp2023 - no female data in either paper.
15. Gapdh as sole reference gene in colon under HFD (authors flag this themselves).

## Relevance to the team
A second, longer (8-wk) PS x HFD 2x2 mouse study whose liver phenotype (higher liver weight, MASLD activity score, Oil Red O area, hepatic TC/TG) supports a "microplastic worsens diet-induced steatosis" prior - but with NO liver transcriptomics, no lipogenic/FAO/oxidative-stress/circadian genes, and again NO formal MP x diet interaction test (one-way ANOVA + Tukey across the four groups). Its ABX and FMT arms are the strongest causal evidence in this batch that the liver/obesity phenotype is microbiota-mediated rather than a direct hepatic particle effect, which is a mechanism worth separating from any PE-driven hepatic transcriptome signal. Note also the direct disagreement with ehp2023 (0.45-0.53 um PS-COOH) on body-weight and liver-weight direction, and that this paper's barrier effects (Muc2, Tjp1, occludin down; Chao1 up) occurred under normal diet too, i.e. not everything is diet-conditional.
