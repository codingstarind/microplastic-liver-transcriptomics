# liverint2025_np_masld

## Citation
Han J, Jeong H, Lee C, Sung A, Choi YH, Jung Y. "Chronic Nanoplastic Exposure Promotes the Development and Progression of Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease." Liver International 2025; 45(8): e70224. DOI 10.1111/liv.70224. PMID 40657663. PMCID PMC12257899. https://doi.org/10.1111/liv.70224
Verbatim (front matter): "Liver International 45, no. 8 (2025): e70224, 10.1111/liv.70224." Jinsol Han, Hayeong Jeong and Chanbin Lee are stated to have contributed equally.

## Access level read
Full text (Europe PMC XML -> PMC12257899.txt) PLUS Supplementary Methods (supp1_methods.txt, extracted from the article's Supporting Information). Methods in the main article are only a pointer: "Methods are described in detail in the Supplementary Methods section." (Section 2 Methods).

## Biological question
Whether chronic oral (drinking-water) exposure to nanoplastics (NPs) promotes development and progression of MASLD/MASH, and by what mechanism (hypothesised: hepatocyte lipotoxic injury -> SHH release -> Hedgehog signalling in hepatic stellate cells -> fibrosis). Intro: "no direct evidence exists on whether the consumption of NPs over a long time influences MASLD progression to MASH."

## Experimental design
2x2 in vivo factorial, 12 weeks: Chow+Veh, Chow+NPs, CDAHFD+Veh, CDAHFD+NPs (Suppl. Methods, Animal experiments). Randomisation stated: "These mice were randomly divided into four groups" (Suppl. Methods). Blinding: not reported (looked in Suppl. Methods "Animal experiments", "Statistical analysis", and main-text Methods). Vehicle control = deionized water in drinking water.
In vitro arms: (i) AML12 mouse hepatocyte line +/- NPs 50 or 100 ug/mL, 24/48 h (Fig. S2); (ii) mouse primary hepatocytes (pHEPs) +/- NPs 50/100 ug/mL (Fig. 1); (iii) pHEPs +/- palmitic acid (PA) 50 uM +/- NPs 100 ug/mL, 24/48 h (Fig. 2A); (iv) quiescent primary HSCs cultured in conditioned medium (CM) from those pHEPs, with control IgG or SHH-neutralising antibody (Fig. 6A,E); (v) pHEPs/pHSCs freshly isolated from the four in vivo groups (Figs. 3G, 7).

## Species/strain/sex/age; tissue(s)
"7-weeks-old male C57BL/6 mice (wild-type; WT) were purchased Hana Biotech" (Suppl. Methods, Animal experiments). Males only; no female arm. Housing: "12-hour light/dark cycle", 22 +/- 1 C, 50 +/- 10% humidity. Tissues: liver (histology, protein, RNA, hydroxyproline, TG, ROS, MDA), serum (ALT/AST), whole-body imaging; isolated primary hepatocytes and primary HSCs. Ethics approval PNU-2020-2574.

## Biological replicates per group
In vivo group sizes (Suppl. Methods): "Chow+Veh (n=6), Chow+NPs (n=12), CDAHFD+Veh (n=6), and CDAHFD+NPs (n=12)" - i.e. UNBALANCED, NP groups twice the size of vehicle groups.
All in vivo figure legends instead report only "n >= 3/group" (Fig. 3B,D,E,F; Fig. 4A,B,C; Fig. 5A; Fig. S3A), so the exact n per assay is NOT reported. Western blots are stated to be "from representative mice per each group" (Fig. 3D legend; Fig. 4C legend).
In vitro: "All data shown represent one of three experiments with similar results" (Figs. 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 legends); Suppl. Methods: "These experiments were repeated at least three times." No RNA-seq in this study, so no sequencing n.

## Polymer; particle size; supplier; label; characterisation; endotoxin
Supplier/catalogue: "100mg NPs (17150-10; Polysciences, Inc., Warrington, PA, USA)" (Suppl. Methods, Cell experiments). Label: "Fluoresbite-labelled NPs" / "Fluoresbite (green)-labelled" (Fig. 1A, Fig. 2A legends); imaged at excitation 465-495 nm, emission 515 nm (Suppl. Methods, in vivo fluorescence imaging).
POLYMER: not explicitly stated for the particles used (the word "polystyrene" appears in this paper only when citing others' work, Introduction ref [7]); inferred polystyrene only from the Polysciences Fluoresbrite catalogue number - NOT VERIFIED from the text.
NOMINAL AND MEASURED SIZE: not reported anywhere in main text or Supplementary Methods (searched for "nm", "diameter", "size", "DLS", "zeta"). The only size statement is a general definition in the Introduction: "microplastics (MPs; 1-5 um) and nanoplastics (NPs; 1-100 nm)". No zeta potential, no DLS, no agglomeration check, no endotoxin/LPS testing reported (searched main text and supplement).

## Dose; route; vehicle; frequency; duration
Route: oral, via drinking water. "mice received drinking water with 1mg/L of NPs or the same volumes of deionized water" (Suppl. Methods, Animal experiments). "The drinking water was changed weekly for 12 weeks." Delivered dose: "each mouse in the NP groups received 0.18ug of NPs /g of BW per day (Figure S1A-D)" (Suppl. Methods). Duration 12 weeks, concurrent with diet. Dose justification: "The administrated dose of NPs in the study was determined based on previous studies in male mice" (Suppl. Methods, refs [1,2]).
Body weight and water intake were measured weekly so that "the same concentration of NPs per BW was given to each mouse for 12 weeks" (Results 3.3); "CDAHFD hardly disturbed the intake of NPs per BW throughout the diet feeding period (Figure S1D)".
In vitro doses: 50 and 100 ug/mL NPs, 24/48 h; PA 50 uM.

## Diet / metabolic-stress model
"Mice fed normal Chow-diet (Chow; M-diet; Optipharm.CO.,LTD, Cheongju, Republic of Korea) or choline-deficient, L-amino acid-defined high fat diet (CDAHFD; A06071302; Research diet, New Brunswick, NJ, USA) consisting 60 kcal% and 0.1% methionine for 12 weeks" (Suppl. Methods, Animal experiments).
NOTE: this is CDAHFD (choline-deficient, methionine-restricted, 60 kcal% fat), NOT a plain high-fat diet; it causes steatohepatitis/fibrosis without obesity. Model rationale (Results 3.3): "the mice exhibited metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver (MAFL) and MASH at 6 and 12 weeks, respectively." The chow control is a standard maintenance diet, i.e. NOT an ingredient-matched/purified control diet.

## Data types
No omics of any kind (no RNA-seq, proteomics, metabolomics, lipidomics, scRNA, spatial). Assays: H&E, Sirius Red, immunohistochemistry (active CASPASE-3, alpha-SMA, GLI2, CD68), TUNEL, Oil Red O; Western blot; qRT-PCR (primers in Table S1); hepatic triglyceride (Cayman colorimetric kit); hydroxyproline; ROS (DCF-DA); MDA (Abcam ab118970); serum ALT/AST (Asan Pharmaceutical AM102-K/AM103-K); MTS cell viability; in vivo fluorescence imaging (CRi Maestro); primary hepatocyte/HSC isolation by two-step collagenase perfusion.

## Statistics & computational methods
Verbatim (Suppl. Methods, Statistical analysis): "Statistically significant differences between the control and treatment groups or subgroups were analyzed with one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by post hoc Tukey's test. Differences were considered as significant when P values were <0.05." Software: IBM SPSS Statistics 21 and GraphPad Prism 8. Data as mean +/- SEM.
INTERACTION TESTING: NO formal diet x NP interaction term was fitted. Although the design is 2x2, the analysis is ONE-WAY ANOVA across the four groups with Tukey pairwise comparisons, and every in vivo figure legend references a single reference group: "*p < 0.05, **p < 0.005 vs. Chow+Veh" (Fig. 3B,D,E,F; Fig. 4A,B,C; Fig. 5A; Fig. S3A). So all "exacerbation"/"synergy" claims rest on pairwise contrasts (and in several places on qualitative wording such as "seem to be higher", "appeared to be higher"), not on an interaction test. No multiple-testing correction beyond Tukey; no exact p-values, effect sizes or confidence intervals are given anywhere - only asterisk thresholds.

## Main findings with effect directions and statistical evidence
IN VITRO (hepatocytes)
- NPs enter AML12 cells and pHEPs (fluorescence), more at 100 than 50 ug/mL; viability unchanged at 24 h but significantly DECREASED at 48 h; cleaved CASPASE-3 UP. (Results 3.1; Fig. S2A-D; Fig. 1B-D) VALIDATED (fluorescence imaging, MTS, Western blot).
- NPs INCREASE lipid droplets (Oil Red O) and UPREGULATE CD36 in AML12 and pHEPs, dose- and time-dependently. (Results 3.1; Fig. 1D,E) VALIDATED (Oil Red O, Western blot).
- NPs INCREASE intracellular ROS and MDA in pHEPs at 48 h: "markedly elevated in pHEPs exposed to NPs at 48 h compared with other groups" (Results 3.1; Fig. 1F,G). VALIDATED (DCF-DA, MDA kit).
- PA + NPs worse than either alone: "ROS production and MDA accumulation were significantly higher in the PA + NPs group than in other groups" (Results 3.2; Fig. 2D,E); cleaved CASPASE-3 "significantly higher in the PA + NPs group than in other treatment groups" (Fig. 2F); CD36 and lipid droplets highest in PA+NPs (Fig. 2F,G). VALIDATED (Western blot, Oil Red O, assays) - but this is a pairwise "greater than each single treatment" claim, NOT a formal interaction test.
IN VIVO (12 weeks)
- NPs accumulate mainly in liver/GI tract, within hepatocytes; in chow mice NP signal was "detected extensively in the torso" whereas in CDAHFD mice "mainly distributed in the gastrointestinal tract and ... especially concentrated in what appeared to be the liver and small intestine" (Results 3.3; Fig. S1E,F). VALIDATED (in vivo fluorescence imaging + liver section fluorescence) but qualitative/semi-quantitative only - no quantified particle burden (no py-GC/MS or particle counting in tissue).
- Chow+NPs mice were essentially UNAFFECTED: "Chow-fed mice with or without NPs showed normal liver morphology and appeared unaffected by chronic exposure to NPs" (Results 3.3); Discussion: "chronic exposure to NPs rarely had apparent pathophysiological effects in mice". VALIDATED (histology). This is the key diet-dependence observation.
- Liver injury markers: CDAHFD significantly INCREASED LW/BW, ALT, AST, but "NP treatment did not provide significant changes" for those (Results 3.3; Fig. 3B) - i.e. NO NP effect on the classic serum injury markers.
- Hepatic TG: "higher levels of hepatic triglycerides (TG) in the CDAHFD groups than the Chow groups were significantly increased by NP treatment" (Results 3.3; Fig. 3C). VALIDATED (TG assay). Direction: UP (lipid accumulation).
- Hepatic CD36 protein: UP with CDAHFD and "significantly elevated by NP exposure in the livers of CDAHFD-fed mice" (Results 3.3; Fig. 3D). VALIDATED (Western blot; also confirmed in isolated pHEPs, Fig. 3G).
- Oxidative stress: "hepatic levels of ROS and MDA were significantly elevated in the CDAHFD+NPs group compared to any other groups" (Results 3.3; Fig. 3E,F). VALIDATED. Direction: UP.
- Apoptosis: active/cleaved CASPASE-3 and TUNEL-positive cells MORE in CDAHFD+NPs than CDAHFD+Veh (Results 3.3; Fig. 3A middle/bottom panels, 3D, 3G). VALIDATED (IHC, TUNEL, Western blot).
- Inflammation: Tnf-a, Il-6, Cxcl1, Cxcl2 mRNA UP in CDAHFD vs Chow, and "NP treatment further elevated the expression of these genes in the CDAHFD-fed mice" (Results 3.3; Fig. S3A). CD68+ macrophages "more apparent in the livers of NP-exposed mice fed CDAHFD" (Fig. S3B). VALIDATED (qRT-PCR, IHC).
- Fibrosis: Tgf-b1, Col1a1, a-Sma mRNA UP with CDAHFD; "the expression of Col1a1 and a-Sma in CDAHFD-fed mice was significantly elevated by NP treatment" (Results 3.4; Fig. 4A). Proteins TGF-b1, COL1a1, a-SMA, VIMENTIN further increased by NPs (Fig. 4B); hydroxyproline increased by NPs in CDAHFD (Fig. 4C); Sirius Red and a-SMA staining increased (Fig. 4D). VALIDATED (qRT-PCR, Western blot, hydroxyproline, histology). Note Tgf-b1 mRNA increase by NPs is not itself declared significant.
- Hedgehog pathway: Shh and Gli2 mRNA UP in CDAHFD and "Chronic exposure to NPs further increased their expression in CDAHFD-fed mice"; "The mRNA level of smoothened (Smo) ... was higher only in the CDAHFD+NPs group than in any other group" (Results 3.4; Fig. 5A). Protein SHH/SMO/GLI2 and GLI2+ cells likewise highest in CDAHFD+NPs (Fig. 5B,C). VALIDATED (qRT-PCR, Western blot, IHC).
- Mechanism / causality: pHEPs treated with NPs+PA produce the most SHH; HSCs in CM from NPs+PA pHEPs show highest proliferation, SMO/GLI2 and fibrotic markers; "the elevated expression of Hh activators and fibrotic markers in HSCs was attenuated when the SHH-neutralising antibody was added to the CM from pHEPs treated with PA + NPs" (Results 3.5; Fig. 6B-E). VALIDATED by antibody-neutralisation rescue (the strongest causal evidence in the paper; it is in vitro, with no in vivo Hh/SHH blockade or knockout).
- Ex vivo confirmation: SHH highest in pHEPs from CDAHFD+NPs; SMO/GLI2 and fibrotic markers in pHSCs from CDAHFD+NPs "seem to be higher" than CDAHFD alone (Results 3.5; Fig. 7A,B) - qualitative wording, blots "represent one of three experiments"; VALIDATED (Western blot) but no statistics reported for Fig. 7.

## Pathway-theme directions
- Lipid metabolism / uptake: UP. Driven by lipid UPTAKE (CD36) rather than de novo lipogenesis; hepatic TG up with NPs on CDAHFD (Fig. 3C,D). The paper explicitly cites (Discussion, ref [35]) that "NP treatment hardly affected the expression of de novo lipogenesis-associated genes, including Srebf1, Fasn, Pparg, Acaca, and Acacb, but elevated the levels of lipid uptake genes, such as Fatp2 and CD36" - that is someone else's finding, not measured here. Fasn/Srebf1/Acly/Pparg were NOT measured in this paper.
- PPARalpha / fatty-acid oxidation (Ppara, Cpt1a, Acox1, Cyp4a): not reported (not measured).
- Cholesterol synthesis: not reported (not measured).
- Inflammation: UP (Tnf-a, Il-6, Cxcl1, Cxcl2 mRNA; CD68+ macrophages) in CDAHFD+NPs vs CDAHFD+Veh (Fig. S3). NF-kB and NLRP3 not measured.
- Oxidative stress: UP (hepatic ROS, MDA highest in CDAHFD+NPs; Fig. 3E,F). Nrf2, Hmox1, SOD, GSH not measured in this paper.
- Mitochondrial function: not measured (mitochondrial dysregulation is only discussed as general MASLD biology in the Introduction).
- Fibrosis: UP (Col1a1, a-Sma, TGF-b1, VIMENTIN, hydroxyproline, Sirius Red) - the most emphasised NP effect; "aggravated CDAHFD-induced hepatic damage, especially fibrosis" (Abstract Results).
- Circadian genes: not reported (not measured).
- MP/NP x diet interaction: CLAIMED but NOT FORMALLY TESTED. The interaction claim is verbal/pairwise: "these particles did not cause much harm on their own when the animals ate a normal diet. However, when the animals had an unhealthy diet ... nanoplastics significantly made the damage and scarring much worse" (lay Summary); "NPs assist in the detrimental action of CDAHFD in mice" (Discussion). Analysis = one-way ANOVA + Tukey across 4 groups, all asterisks "vs. Chow+Veh" - no interaction term, no two-way ANOVA mentioned.

## Author-acknowledged limitations
- Mechanism upstream of CD36 unknown: "However, it remains to be seen how NPs altered CD36 expression in the research." (Discussion, para 2). And: "further studies are essential to elucidate the detailed mechanisms through which hepatic NP deposition induces oxidative stress to lipid metabolic disturbances." (Discussion, para 2).
- Direction of causality for hepatic accumulation unresolved: "It is veiled whether plastic particles induce liver disease or if the damaged liver cannot properly remove plastic particles, resulting in the accumulation of plastic particles." and "further studies are essential to determine whether lysosomal dysfunction caused by lipotoxicity facilitates the accumulation of ingested plastic particles in the liver." (Discussion, para 3).
- Human relevance limited by detection methods: "these physiological impacts of NPs have been based on animal studies, not human ones, because accumulated NPs in human tissues cannot be identified due to a lack of in situ detection techniques" (Discussion, para 1).
- Diet-dependence acknowledged as a scope limit: "given that chronic exposure to NPs rarely had apparent pathophysiological effects in mice, NPs could pose a greater risk to individuals whose eating habits make them more susceptible to MASLD." (Discussion, para 3).
No explicit limitations paragraph exists; the above are the only limitation-type statements (searched Discussion and Conclusions).

## Methodological points to verify independently
(Questions for the reader, not accusations.)
1. n mismatch: allocation says Chow+Veh n=6, Chow+NPs n=12, CDAHFD+Veh n=6, CDAHFD+NPs n=12, but every in vivo legend says only "n >= 3/group". Which mice entered each assay? Were NP groups larger because of a planned interim sacrifice ("At the end of each time point, mice were sacrificed")? Is the analysed n the number of mice or pooled samples?
2. Unbalanced 2x2 (6/12/6/12) plus one-way ANOVA + Tukey: would a two-way ANOVA with a diet x NP interaction term support the "exacerbation" claim? Several comparisons are described in words ("seem to be higher", "appeared to be higher") without statistics - e.g. Fig. 7A,B has no stated test.
3. Reference group: all asterisks are "vs. Chow+Veh". Which CDAHFD+NPs vs CDAHFD+Veh contrasts were actually significant, and at what p?
4. Particle characterisation: polymer type, nominal/measured diameter, dispersity, zeta potential, aggregation in drinking water and in culture medium, surfactant/azide content of the stock suspension, and endotoxin are all unreported. Does the Fluoresbrite label leach? Was a label-free or differently-labelled particle control used?
5. Dose traceability: 1 mg/L in drinking water -> "0.18 ug of NPs/g BW per day". Does that back-calculate from the reported water intake and BW (Fig. S1A-D)? Water bottles were "changed weekly" - was particle settling/adhesion to the bottle checked?
6. Diet control: chow (grain-based M-diet) vs CDAHFD (purified, 60 kcal% fat, 0.1% methionine, choline-deficient) are not ingredient-matched; part of the Chow-vs-CDAHFD difference is diet formulation, not just fat/choline. Also CDAHFD is non-obesogenic, so this is not a classical HFD/obesity MASLD model.
7. Housing/cage structure: cages per group, cage as experimental unit, and whether NP and vehicle mice were co-housed are not reported. Drinking water is a cage-level exposure, so cage may be the real unit of randomisation.
8. Sex: males only (7 weeks old). Note that the same group previously published sex-specific MASH differences (Lee et al., Nat Commun 2022, cited as ref [3] in the supplement), so sex-generalisability is untested here.
9. Blinding of histology scoring / TUNEL counting / densitometry: not reported. Histology appears qualitative (representative images); no NAS/fibrosis stage scoring by a blinded pathologist is reported.
10. Western blots are "from representative mice per each group" - how many mice per blot, and do the densitometry bars aggregate independent mice or technical replicates? "one of three experiments with similar results" for in vivo tissue blots needs clarifying.
11. No molecular endpoints distinguishing NP effects from food-intake/caloric differences: food intake per group is not reported (only BW and water intake, Fig. S1A-B).
12. Batch structure: all 12-week endpoints, one cohort? Not stated.

## Relevance to the team
Closest published analogue of the team's 2x2 plastic x diet liver design, but with nanoplastics in drinking water on CDAHFD rather than PE microplastics on a MASH diet, and with NO transcriptomics - all endpoints are protein/histology/qPCR. It is a useful contrast case for the interaction question: the headline "exacerbation" rests entirely on pairwise Tukey contrasts in an unbalanced one-way ANOVA with all asterisks referenced to Chow+Veh, with no diet x particle interaction term - the same inferential gap the team must avoid. Its directionally usable claims for a MASLD-plus-plastic liver signature are: CD36/lipid uptake UP, hepatic TG UP, ROS/MDA UP, Tnf-a/Il-6/Cxcl1/Cxcl2 UP, Col1a1/a-Sma/TGF-b1 UP, Shh/Smo/Gli2 UP, with no effect on ALT/AST/LW:BW and essentially no phenotype under chow.
