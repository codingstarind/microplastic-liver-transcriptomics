## Biological question
Does developmental stage (juvenile vs adult) determine susceptibility to polystyrene-nanoplastic (PS-NP) exacerbation of high-fat-diet-induced MAFLD, and is the mechanism a disruption of the hepatic circadian-mitochondrial-AMPK axis? Framed under the DOHaD paradigm: "whether developmental stage determines susceptibility to PS-NPs-induced metabolic disturbances under dietary stress" (Introduction).

## Experimental design
- In vivo: two age cohorts (3-week-old "juvenile", 8-week-old "adult" male C57BL/6J at start of exposure). Within EACH age cohort a 2x2 factorial: diet (normal chow vs HFD 60% kcal fat) x PS-NPs (0 vs 20 ug/mL in drinking water), 10 weeks. Groups named NC, PS, HFD, HP (= HFD + PS-NPs). Quote (Results 2.1): "mice were subjected to a 2 x 2 factorial experimental design combining dietary intervention ... and PS-NPs exposure (0 or 20 ug/mL in drinking water; 100-nm GFP-labeled particles) for 10 weeks".
- Randomisation: "Animals were randomly divided into four treatment groups" (Methods 4.5); "Group allocation was randomized" (Methods 4.23 Statistical Analysis).
- Blinding: "histopathological assessment, image quantification, and downstream data analysis were conducted without knowledge of treatment assignment" (Methods 4.23); NAS scored by "A blinded pathologist" (Methods 4.13).
- Housing: group-housed; "Because the animals were group-housed, consumption was recorded for each cage" (Methods 4.2). Number of mice per cage: not reported.
- In vivo pharmacological rescue (separate arm): juvenile HP mice given AICAR 500 mg/kg/day or SR9009 100 mg/kg/day by oral gavage during the final 4 weeks of the 10-week protocol; vehicle controls. "Dosing was performed at a fixed Zeitgeber time" (Methods 4.6) - the actual ZT is not reported.
- In vitro: HepG2 and Huh7 cells, 2x2 factorial FFA x PS-NPs (48 h), plus AICAR and SR9009 rescue arms and dexamethasone-synchronised 48 h circadian time courses (sampling every 4 h, 13 time points 0-48 h).

## Species/strain/sex/age; tissue(s)
Male C57BL/6J mice only (Shanghai SLAC). Juvenile = 3 weeks old at start; adult = 8 weeks old at start; both exposed 10 weeks. Sex is male only - authors acknowledge this as a limitation.
Housing: 25 +/- 1 C, 50-60% RH, 12 h light/12 h dark, lights on 06:00 = ZT0 (Methods 4.1).
Tissues: liver (RNA-seq, RT-qPCR, western blot, H&E, Oil Red O, TEM, isolated mitochondria for JC-1 and CPT1 activity, ATP/ADP), serum (ALT, AST, LDL-C, HDL-C, TG, T-CHO). Adipose only as "adiposity index". Human hepatocyte lines HepG2 and Huh7 in vitro (STR-authenticated, mycoplasma-monitored).

## Biological replicates per group (per assay)
- Phenotype / biochemistry / histology / mitochondrial function: n = 6 per group. "Animal experiments included six mice in each group" (Methods 4.23); Figure 1 legend "n = 6/group"; Fig. 4 legend "n = 6 for c-g".
- RNA-seq: n = 5 per group. Figure 2 and Figure 3 legends: "(n = 5/group)". Methods 4.15: "RNA-seq was conducted on 40 liver samples" and "The dataset comprised 40 libraries" - consistent with 8 groups (2 ages x 4 treatments) x 5.
- Western blot (liver): n = 5. Fig. 4 legend "n = 5 for a,b"; Fig. 9 legend "n = 5 for b".
- In vitro: n = 3 for immunoblots/time-course protein (Fig. 5c,d; Fig. 7a; Fig. 8a,b); n = 6 for most functional assays (Fig. 5e,f; Fig. 6; Fig. 7b-g; Fig. 8c-i).
- Unit of analysis stated: "Independent animals or independently prepared cell cultures were regarded as biological replicates and constituted the units used for statistical testing" (Methods 4.23). Note: that makes the mouse, not the cage, the unit - see "points to verify".

## Polymer; particle size; supplier; surface/fluorescent label; characterisation; endotoxin
- Polymer: polystyrene nanoplastics (PS-NPs), GFP/fluorescent-labelled.
- Supplier/catalogue: "Fluorescent polystyrene nanoplastics (PS-NPs-GFP; nominal diameter 100 nm, FF3301G) were purchased from Rigor Biotechnology" (Methods 4.4).
- Nominal size 100 nm. Measured: DLS hydrodynamic diameter "115.4 +/- 1.5 nm" with PDI "0.041 +/- 0.040"; zeta potential "-30.9 +/- 0.7 mV" (Results 2.1 and Methods 4.4; Zetasizer Nano ZS90, software v7.3.0). SEM confirmed morphology/primary size and showed no aggregation over 3 days (Figure S2a - supplementary figure, not read; the numbers above are quoted from the main text).
- Pre-use handling: "vortexed and sonicated (40 kHz, 10 min)"; drinking water sonicated twice daily for 30 min and replaced every 3 days (Methods 4.4).
- Stability during exposure: water sampled daily and examined by SEM, "No detectable aggregation or sedimentation was observed over the 3-day replacement cycle" (Methods 4.4).
- Label control: "Comparable biological responses between GFP-labeled and unlabeled PS-NPs suggest that fluorescent labeling did not substantially influence the observed metabolic effects" (Discussion). The supporting data for that comparison are not shown in the main text (likely Figure S5 - not read).
- ENDOTOXIN: not reported anywhere (searched Methods 4.4, 4.5 and whole text). Surfactant/residual-monomer content: not reported.

## Dose; route; vehicle; frequency; duration
- Dose as reported: 20 ug/mL PS-NPs in drinking water (in vivo); 20 ug/mL in vitro (and 5 ug/mL as a lower in-vitro dose in Figs 5-6).
- Route: oral, ad libitum drinking water. Vehicle: drinking water (in vitro vehicle controls: PBS or DMSO <= 0.1%).
- Frequency/duration: continuous, exposure solution replaced every 3 days, for 10 weeks.
- Estimated internal dose (cage-level estimate, first 8 weeks): "the calculated mean weekly PS-NPs intake was 25.53, 21.69, 24.17, and 24.81 ug/g body weight/week in juvenile PS-NPs, juvenile HFD + PS-NPs (HP), adult PS-NPs, and adult HP groups"; cumulative "204.24, 173.56, 193.33, and 198.46 ug/g body weight" (Results 2.1, citing Table S1 - supplementary, not read). Note the estimate covers only the first 8 of 10 weeks; why is not stated.
- Dose rationale: "a concentration chosen based on previous chronic exposure studies and preliminary validation to avoid overt toxicity (Figure S5a)" (Methods 4.5). In vitro: "This concentration represents the highest non-cytotoxic dose" (Methods 4.5).
- Rescue drugs: AICAR 500 mg/kg/day, SR9009 100 mg/kg/day, oral gavage, final 4 weeks (Methods 4.6); in vitro AICAR 0.25-2 mM (Selleck S1802), SR9009 0.3125-20 uM (MedChemExpress HY-16989), 48 h.

## Diet / metabolic-stress model
- HFD: "HFD-fed mice received a 60% kcal fat diet (Research Diets, D12492)" (Methods 4.5). This is the classic 60% kcal lard HFD, i.e. an obesogenic steatosis model, NOT a MASH/CDAHFD/western-diet-plus-fructose model.
- Control diet: "standard chow" - brand, composition and % kcal fat NOT reported, and it is not a purified low-fat control matched to D12492 (e.g. D12450). This is a point to verify (below).
- In vitro lipotoxic stress: FFA mixture "500 uM sodium oleate plus 250 uM sodium palmitate" for 48 h (Results 2.4 and Fig. 5 legend).
- Energy intake: "HFD feeding significantly increased energy intake compared with chow-fed controls, whereas PS-NPs exposure did not further affect caloric consumption in either age group (Figure S1c)".

## Data types
- Bulk liver RNA-seq: 40 libraries; "Strand-specific libraries were prepared from 1 ug total RNA using the Illumina Stranded mRNA Prep kit and sequenced on a DNBSEQ-T7 platform (PE150)" (Methods 4.15). Depth: "a median of 43.78 million clean reads per library (range, 39.41-56.26 million)"; "Median overall and unique alignment rates were 96.93% (range, 93.51%-98.92%) and 74.55% (range, 60.99%-85.82%)". Batch: "All libraries were constructed and sequenced within the same batch; therefore, no batch-effect correction was applied."
- RT-qPCR (liver and cells), 2^-ddCt, beta-actin reference, primers in Table S2 (not read).
- Western blot: BMAL1, AMPKalpha, p-AMPKalpha(Thr172), beta-actin (antibody catalogue numbers in Methods 4.16).
- Histology: H&E with blinded NAS; Oil Red O with ImageJ area quantification; TEM for mitochondrial area and cristae density.
- Functional/biochemical: serum biochemistry (Hitachi 7180), hepatic TG/T-CHO colorimetric, ATP (luciferase) and ADP (Amplex Red) -> ATP/ADP ratio, JC-1 membrane potential on ISOLATED liver mitochondria and on cells, CPT1 activity (colorimetric kit ml301014, DTNB/TNB, nmol/min/mg mito protein), Seahorse XFe96 mito stress test (cells only), H2DCFDA ROS (cells only), GTT/ITT.
- No proteomics, metabolomics, lipidomics, scRNA-seq or spatial data.

## Statistics & computational methods
- DE: "Differential expression was analyzed with DESeq2 using raw count data" on RSEM counts; threshold "|log2 fold change| >= 1 and adjusted p < 0.05" (Fig. 2 legend). Pipeline: fastp -> HISAT2 to GRCm39 -> RSEM; TPM used only for visualisation.
- Enrichment: "GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed with GOATOOLS and SciPy, respectively, with Bonferroni correction" (Methods 4.15). Reactome enrichment is shown in Fig. 3c,d but the tool/threshold for Reactome is NOT reported.
- IMPORTANT (transcriptomics design): the RNA-seq is analysed as PAIRWISE DESeq2 contrasts between groups (Fig. 2b "in each indicated comparison"), NOT as a factorial model with a diet x PS-NPs interaction term. No interaction term, no DEG counts, and no overlap/epistasis statistics are reported in the main text. The "amplification" claim for transcriptomics (Results 2.2) rests on comparing numbers/strength of DEGs between separate pairwise contrasts, which is an informal comparison, not a formal interaction test.
- Phenotype statistics DO use a formal factorial model: "For factorial experiments, two-way ANOVA was applied, with diet or FFA treatment and PS-NPs exposure or concentration entered as the two factors" (Methods 4.23); figure legends for Figs 1, 4, 5(a,b,f), 6 state "Two-way ANOVA (HFD x PS-NPs) with Tukey correction". However, the figure legends report only pairwise asterisks; the F and p values for the interaction term are NOT given in the main text for the in vivo phenotypes (only for the in vitro circadian time courses, which are referred to Table S3 - not read).
- In vitro time courses: "two-way ANOVA incorporating treatment, sampling time, and the treatment x time interaction. The corresponding F and p values for the main and interaction effects are provided in Table S3."
- Rescue experiments (Figs 7, 8, 9): one-way ANOVA with Tukey.
- Normality/variance: Shapiro-Wilk and Levene. Two-sided, p < 0.05.
- Age is NOT entered as a factor: juvenile and adult are analysed as separate 2x2 experiments, so "age-dependence" is a comparison of two separate models, not a formally tested three-way (age x diet x PS-NPs) interaction. Quote supporting this reading: "Comparative analysis between age groups demonstrated that alterations ... were substantially more pronounced in juvenile than adult mice" (Results 2.3) - comparative wording, no test statistic.

## Main findings with effect directions and statistical evidence
(VALIDATED = orthogonal non-omics assay; this does NOT imply genetic causality unless stated.)

1. Liver index: PS-NPs alone no effect; HFD raised it at both ages; PS-NPs added to HFD raised it further ONLY in juveniles. VALIDATED (organ weight, n=6, two-way ANOVA HFD x PS-NPs, Fig. 1b). "additional PS-NPs exposure further increased liver index only in juvenile mice" (Results 2.1).
2. Serum ALT and AST INCREASED in juvenile HP vs HFD alone; no further increase in adults. VALIDATED (Fig. 1c): "serum ALT and AST levels were significantly elevated in juvenile HP mice compared with mice receiving HFD alone".
3. Serum LDL-C, HDL-C, TG, T-CHO: PS-NPs AMPLIFIED the HFD-induced INCREASE, juveniles only. VALIDATED (Fig. 1d-f): "PS-NPs co-exposure significantly amplified HFD-induced elevations in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), triglycerides (TG), and total cholesterol (T-CHO)".
4. Histology: juvenile HP had more severe steatosis, ballooning, vacuolar degeneration, higher NAS and Oil Red O area vs HFD; adults showed "limited additional pathological changes". VALIDATED (blinded H&E/NAS, Oil Red O, Fig. 1g,h).
5. Body weight gain, adiposity, GTT and ITT NOT further altered by PS-NPs (Figure S3a-d, supplementary not read). Quote from Results 2.1: "PS-NPs did not further aggravate HFD-induced glucose intolerance or significantly alter insulin sensitivity". The liver phenotype is therefore dissociated from obesity and systemic glycaemia.
6. Intake and hepatic particle load comparable across ages (Figures S1, S2c, not read) - used to argue equal exposure. Intake is cage-level, not per-mouse.
7. Transcriptome: juvenile co-exposure gave "substantially enhanced gene expression remodeling" vs single exposures; adults attenuated. OMICS-ONLY, and NOT formally tested as an interaction: no DEG counts in the main text, only top-25 up/down heatmaps per pairwise contrast (Fig. 2a,b).
8. Bmal1 and Npas2 mRNA: DOWN with PS-NPs alone and DOWN with HFD alone, then "partially restored under co-exposure relative to HFD alone" - NON-MONOTONIC. OMICS-ONLY (Fig. 2b). Authors caution: "Given that profiling was performed at a single circadian time point, these patterns likely reflect feedback regulation or phase-dependent transcriptional variability rather than restoration of circadian homeostasis."
9. Enrichment (juveniles): single exposures -> circadian rhythm and AMPK signalling; co-exposure -> oxidative phosphorylation and metabolic pathways; Reactome co-exposure -> lipid metabolism, mitochondrial FA beta-oxidation, respiratory electron transport. Adults: HFD -> OXPHOS and lipid metabolism, PS-NPs adding "only marginal additional pathway alterations". OMICS-ONLY/COMPUTATIONAL (Fig. 3a-d). Up vs down direction of these pathways is not stated in the text.
10. BMAL1 PROTEIN in liver: DECREASED by PS-NPs alone and by HFD alone, INCREASED under combined exposure at the sampled time point. VALIDATED (western blot, n=5, Fig. 4a) but non-monotonic; authors refuse a restoration reading: "this increase likely reflects altered circadian phase, compensatory feedback regulation, or temporal misalignment rather than restoration of normal circadian function." Also "the persistent faster-migrating BMAL1 protein band ... raises the possibility of altered post-translational modification" (Discussion).
11. AMPK: p-AMPK REDUCED by either single exposure and FURTHER SUPPRESSED under combined exposure (monotonic). VALIDATED (western blot p-AMPKalpha/t-AMPKalpha, Fig. 4b).
12. Energy state: hepatic ATP/ADP ratio DECREASED (Fig. 4c); hepatic ATP DECREASED and mitochondrial membrane potential DECREASED under co-exposure (Fig. 4f,g). VALIDATED (luciferase ATP, Amplex Red ADP, JC-1 on isolated mitochondria).
13. CPT1 activity DECREASED in juvenile HP vs HFD alone; in adults "the reduction was modest and statistically insignificant" (Fig. 4d). VALIDATED (enzyme activity on liver mitochondrial extracts). Authors caution it is not isoform-specific: "the measured activity was conservatively described as CPT1 activity rather than CPT1A-specific activity", and "CPT1 activity ... does not directly represent the dynamic process of mitochondrial FAO".
14. TEM: swelling after either single exposure; co-exposure -> REDUCED CRISTAE DENSITY with "partial alleviation of mitochondrial swelling" (Fig. 4e). VALIDATED, again non-monotonic.
15. Liver RT-qPCR (Fig. 4h): lipogenic Fasn, Lxra, Srebp1c PROGRESSIVELY UP with co-exposure. FAO/OXPHOS genes Acadl, Pgc-1a, Cpt1a, Atp5a1, Ndufa9, Sdha BIPHASIC - "increased expression following individual stressors but decreased expression after combined exposure". VALIDATED (qPCR).
16. In vitro (HepG2, Huh7): PS-NPs + FFA INCREASED lipid droplets and TG vs FFA alone. VALIDATED (Oil Red O, TG, Fig. 5a,b).
17. In vitro BMAL1 protein was INCREASED by PS-NPs alone and by FFA alone - the OPPOSITE direction to the in vivo single-exposure result - with the most pronounced effect under co-exposure; p-AMPK reduced. VALIDATED (western blot, Fig. 5c). The paper does not reconcile this in vivo / in vitro direction discrepancy.
18. Dexamethasone-synchronised time course: co-exposure BLUNTED BMAL1 oscillatory amplitude with "sustained suppression of BMAL1 protein", plus altered phase; mRNA time course likewise altered. VALIDATED (13 time points over 48 h, two-way ANOVA with a formal treatment x time INTERACTION term, Fig. 5d,e; F/p in Table S3, not read). This is the only formal interaction test explicitly described in the paper.
19. Mitochondrial function in vitro: co-exposure DECREASED membrane potential and ATP, INCREASED ROS; Seahorse showed decreased ATP-linked OCR and maximal respiration. VALIDATED (JC-1, ATP, H2DCFDA, Seahorse XFe96, Fig. 6a-d). Author caveat: "absolute proton leak OCR was decreased rather than increased ... should be interpreted cautiously".
20. Acadl, Pgc-1a, Atp5a1, Ndufa9 DOWN under co-exposure in cells (Fig. 6e). VALIDATED (qPCR), direction consistent with the in vivo combined-exposure arm.
21. AICAR RESCUE in vitro: raised p-AMPK dose-dependently; raised membrane potential and ATP; raised ATP-linked and maximal OCR; lowered ROS; lowered lipid droplets and TG. VALIDATED by PHARMACOLOGICAL RESCUE only (one-way ANOVA, Fig. 7). No genetic AMPK manipulation anywhere in the paper.
22. SR9009 (REV-ERB agonist) RESCUE in vitro: lowered BMAL1 dose-dependently; "partially restored both oscillatory amplitude and phase dynamics"; raised membrane potential, ATP, ATP-linked/maximal respiration, spare capacity, coupling efficiency; lowered ROS, lipid and TG (Fig. 8). VALIDATED by PHARMACOLOGICAL RESCUE. A second agonist, SR9011, reportedly reproduced the mitochondrial benefit (Figure S4a-d, supplementary, not read) - a useful second-compound control that the main text only asserts.
23. IN VIVO rescue, juvenile HP mice: target engagement shown (p-AMPK up with AICAR, BMAL1 down with SR9009, Fig. 9b). Both drugs reduced body-weight gain. AICAR lowered liver index, adiposity index, hepatic lipid, ALT and AST. SR9009 lowered liver index and ALT but NOT AST, with "comparatively limited" effects on adiposity and steatosis. Neither changed serum TG or T-CHO. Both raised hepatic membrane potential and ATP and lowered NAS. VALIDATED by IN VIVO PHARMACOLOGICAL RESCUE (n=6, one-way ANOVA, Fig. 9c-j).
24. Dissociation claim: SR9009 protected mitochondria strongly while barely reducing steatosis; AICAR did the reverse. The authors read this as "a distributed regulatory network model". This is an INFERENCE from pharmacological dissociation, not a directly tested mechanism.
25. NOT DONE anywhere in this paper: genetic loss- or gain-of-function of any kind (no Bmal1 / Nr1d1 / Prkaa knockout, knockdown, liver-specific deletion or overexpression), no ChIP, no FAO flux or isotope tracing, no acylcarnitine profiling, no in vivo circadian time-series sampling. The authors say so: "Genetic validation using tissue-specific loss-of-function or gain-of-function models will be essential to establish definitive causal relationships."

## Pathway-theme directions
- Lipid / lipogenesis: UP under combined exposure. Fasn, Lxra, Srebp1c progressively up in juvenile liver (qPCR, VALIDATED); hepatic TG and T-CHO up; Oil Red O area up; FAS and LXRalpha altered in cells.
- PPAR-alpha / FAO: NOT directly measured. Ppara itself is never assayed (no Ppara, Acox1 or Cyp4a data in this paper). The FAO axis is represented by Cpt1a and Acadl mRNA, Pgc-1a mRNA and CPT1 enzyme activity. Direction: UP after a single stressor, DOWN after combined exposure (biphasic); CPT1 ACTIVITY DOWN in juvenile HP vs HFD (VALIDATED). So the net combined-exposure direction is SUPPRESSED FAO capacity.
- Cholesterol synthesis: not assayed as a pathway. Only serum T-CHO and hepatic T-CHO content (both UP with co-exposure in juveniles, VALIDATED). No Hmgcr / Sqle / Srebf2 data.
- Inflammation: essentially NOT studied. No Tnf, Il1b, Il6, NF-kB, NLRP3 or macrophage-marker measurements anywhere. The only inflammatory read-out is the lobular-inflammation component inside the composite NAS score (H&E). Treat this paper as uninformative on MP-induced hepatic inflammation.
- Oxidative stress: ROS UP in hepatocytes under co-exposure, reduced by AICAR and SR9009 (H2DCFDA, VALIDATED, in vitro only). NO in vivo oxidative-stress measurement at all - no MDA, SOD, GSH, Nrf2 or Hmox1 in liver.
- Mitochondria: the best-supported theme. Combined exposure -> reduced cristae density (TEM), reduced membrane potential (JC-1), reduced ATP and ATP/ADP, reduced CPT1 activity in vivo; reduced ATP-linked and maximal respiration in vitro (Seahorse). Transcript level is biphasic and the authors explicitly warn that transcriptional activation "may represent an adaptive response to energetic stress rather than preserved mitochondrial function".
- Fibrosis: NOT studied. No Sirius red, no Col1a1/Acta2/Timp1, no hydroxyproline. NAS does not include fibrosis stage.
- Circadian: core clock perturbed but NON-MONOTONICALLY at the single sampled time point (Bmal1/Npas2 mRNA down with single exposures, partly up with co-exposure; BMAL1 protein down with single exposures, up with co-exposure). Rhythmicity itself (amplitude and phase) was only assessed IN VITRO after dexamethasone synchronisation, where co-exposure blunted amplitude and shifted phase.
- MP x diet interaction: claimed as "exacerbation" throughout. For PHENOTYPES the design and the stated model are a proper 2x2 two-way ANOVA with HFD and PS-NPs as factors (so an interaction term exists in the model), but the main text and legends report only Tukey pairwise asterisks - no interaction F or p value is shown for any in vivo phenotype. For TRANSCRIPTOMICS there is no interaction model at all: DESeq2 pairwise contrasts only. The age effect is likewise a comparison of two separate 2x2 experiments, not a tested three-way interaction.

## Author-acknowledged limitations (quotes + location)
All from the Discussion (final three paragraphs).
1. Exposure quantification: "Because mice were group-housed and exposed through drinking water, individual PS-NPs intake could not be directly quantified." And: "it cannot fully exclude individual differences in drinking behavior, gastrointestinal absorption, biodistribution, and tissue accumulation."
2. Single formulation: "only one PS-NPs formulation, particle size, and exposure concentration were investigated."
3. Environmental relevance of the dose: "its quantitative relevance to real-world human exposure requires further evaluation using environmentally representative concentrations and exposure mixtures."
4. Circadian assessment: "circadian alterations were primarily characterized using selected time points and in vitro models, limiting comprehensive evaluation of long-term rhythmic changes in vivo."
5. Sex: "this study was performed exclusively in male mice; therefore, potential sex-dependent differences in susceptibility and metabolic responses remain to be investigated."
6. Single-time-point clock measurement: "interpretation of this single-time-point measurement requires caution because circadian function is determined by dynamic parameters, including phase, amplitude, and periodicity."
7. Cell-model limits: "immortalized hepatocyte models cannot fully reproduce systemic circadian entrainment signals derived from hormonal, neural, and feeding cues."
8. Transcriptome vs function: "transcriptional activation of mitochondrial pathways may represent an adaptive response to energetic stress rather than preserved mitochondrial function. These findings highlight the importance of integrating transcriptomic and functional analyses".
9. CPT1 assay: "the commercial assay used in this study was not validated for isoform-specific detection".
10. Drug specificity and the need for genetics: "off-target or pathway-independent effects of AICAR and REV-ERB agonists cannot be completely excluded"; "Genetic validation using tissue-specific loss-of-function or gain-of-function models will be essential to establish definitive causal relationships."
11. Unexplained BMAL1 band: "the molecular mechanisms underlying this observation remain to be determined."

## Methodological points to verify independently
(Questions, not accusations.)
- Unit of analysis vs cage: Methods 4.23 declares the individual animal the statistical unit, but exposure is delivered through shared drinking water in group housing and intake is recorded per cage (Methods 4.2). Is treatment confounded with cage, as in the team's own dataset? How many mice per cage, and how many cages per group? Not reported.
- Chow control: the HFD is a purified 60% kcal fat diet (D12492) but the control is unspecified "standard chow". Was a matched purified low-fat control (e.g. D12450H/J) used? Fibre, sucrose and micronutrient differences between chow and purified diets are a known confounder for liver transcriptomes.
- Interaction statistics: the model is two-way ANOVA, so interaction F and p exist - are they reported for the in vivo phenotypes anywhere (Table S3 or source data)? The main text shows only pairwise asterisks. Which claims survive on the interaction term rather than on PS vs HP pairwise contrasts?
- Transcriptomic interaction: DEGs are pairwise DESeq2 contrasts. Could a ~diet*PSNP model be fitted to the 40 libraries? "Amplification" based on comparing DEG list sizes between contrasts is sensitive to within-group variance, which differs between groups.
- Missing DEG counts: no DEG numbers are given for any contrast in the main text. Where are they (Fig. 2a panel, supplementary tables)?
- ZT control (relevant to the team's clock-phase confound): this study DOES control sampling time - "euthanasia, and terminal tissue collection were performed within the same circadian window (ZT4-ZT6)" (Methods 4.8), with 7 days acclimation on 12:12 LD, lights on 06:00 = ZT0. Worth verifying whether that window applies to the RNA-seq animals specifically, since the sentence is hedged "Unless otherwise indicated". Even with ZT control, a single ZT cannot separate amplitude change from phase shift - the authors concede this.
- Feeding behaviour as a clock cue: HFD shifts the feeding rhythm in mice, which itself phase-shifts the liver clock. Food intake was measured per cage per 3 days, not as a day/night distribution. Was nocturnal vs diurnal food intake measured? Not reported - so diet-driven feeding-time shifts cannot be excluded as the proximate cause of the clock-gene changes.
- Library/batch: authors state one batch, no correction. Were depth or alignment rate associated with group? The reported unique alignment rate range is wide (60.99%-85.82%) - is it balanced across groups? Not shown.
- RNA-seq n=5 vs phenotype n=6: which 5 of 6 mice per group were sequenced, and how were they chosen?
- Normalisation of qPCR to beta-actin alone: a single reference gene under a condition (HFD + nanoplastic) known to alter cytoskeletal and metabolic transcripts. Was reference-gene stability checked?
- Figure 6 legend says "Two-way ANOVA (HFD x PS-NPs)" for a pure cell-culture experiment where the factor is FFA, not HFD - almost certainly a legend copy-paste, but worth confirming which factors were actually modelled.
- Particle characterisation gaps: endotoxin content NOT reported; no measurement of free surfactant, residual monomer or leachates; GFP label is a protein tag on the particle, and protein coronas differ with label - the unlabelled-particle comparison is asserted in the Discussion but its data sit in supplementary figures.
- Dose estimate covers "the first eight weeks" of a 10-week exposure - why not all 10?
- In vivo rescue arms: is the comparison group for AICAR/SR9009 a vehicle-gavaged HP group? Methods say "controls received vehicle", but one-way ANOVA across the rescue panel means gavage stress is shared; confirm all arms were gavaged.
- Direction conflict for BMAL1 protein between in vivo single exposures (down) and in vitro single exposures (up) is not addressed; check whether the in vitro result is time-of-harvest dependent (48 h post-treatment, unsynchronised in Fig. 5c).

## Relevance to the team
Closest published analogue to the team's design: a 2x2 factorial of oral nanoplastic x high-fat diet with bulk liver RNA-seq (n=5/group) plus functional validation, and it reports the MP x diet "exacerbation" the team is testing. Two directly usable contrasts with Jung et al.: (i) sampling time is controlled here (all collections in a ZT4-ZT6 window with 7 days LD acclimation), which is the control the team's clock-phase confound lacks, and the authors still refuse to read a single-time-point clock measurement as rhythm disruption; (ii) the exacerbation claim is supported by two-way-ANOVA-modelled phenotypes but the transcriptomic "amplification" rests on pairwise DESeq2 contrasts with no interaction term and no reported DEG counts. Also note it is PS (polystyrene, 100 nm) not PE, 60% kcal fat not a MASH diet, male-only, group-housed with cage-level intake, and with no public sequence accession.
